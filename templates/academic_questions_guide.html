Answering questions about your academic record

State the facts, give a brief explanation, then describe what you learned or changed. Close with something that helps the interviewer assess your readiness for the role.

Be honest and adapt these outlines to your own experience. Share only the personal details you are comfortable discussing. Keep dates, grades, and backlog status consistent with your records.

Gap year

Structure. Name the period, explain why you took time away in a sentence, and describe what you did before returning or preparing for work.

  • Do. Mention a genuine responsibility, activity, or step that helped you resume your plans.
  • Avoid. Filling the gap with invented courses, jobs, or achievements.

Example outline. Dates of the gap → family responsibilities, if applicable → the course or project you actually resumed → what that work shows about your readiness.

Backlogs

Structure. Say which backlogs are still active or have been cleared, briefly explain what happened, and describe how you are addressing or addressed them.

  • Do. Give a verified result or the actual next exam date if you know it.
  • Avoid. Calling an active backlog cleared or predicting a result you cannot confirm.

Example outline. One course still active → what made it difficult → the weekly practice you started → the next scheduled attempt, if confirmed.

Low CGPA

Structure. Give the official CGPA and scale, explain a relevant factor, and point to more recent evidence of your skills.

  • Do. Use a real project, later result, or work sample and name your contribution.
  • Avoid. Rounding up, leaving out the scale, or blaming others for the result.

Example outline. Actual CGPA out of ten → early difficulty with a subject → the study change you made → a recent project that demonstrates a relevant skill.
